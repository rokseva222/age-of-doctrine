1446|Welcome, commander
1447|This short guide teaches the basics of the game. You can skip any step with “Skip”; action steps advance by themselves when you do them.
1448|Learn the map
1449|Drag the map with your finger and pinch with two fingers to zoom. Now tap any province.
1450|Province card
1451|The card shows the province's owner, population, income and defense; the capital has a star. Your provinces bring you income, and you lose income as you lose them.
1452|Top bar
1453|Treasury (money), troops, province count and date are here. Each turn is one week: income is added to your treasury every week, while army upkeep and spending are deducted.
1454|Select your army
1455|Tap one of your own provinces and press “Select army” on the card. Armies appear on the map with their troop counts.
1456|Move the army
1457|Tap a neighboring province and press “March army here”. An army you send by hand waits on arrival; armies you give no orders to fight automatically if that setting is on.
1458|End the turn
1459|Advance one week with the “End turn” button at the top right. News flows in the World Bulletin at the top; tap it to open the history.
1460|Policy panel
1461|The tank button on the right: budget split (military, industry, cyber, logistics...), arms purchases, inventory, trade, fronts, alliances, intelligence and cabinet. Your strength grows here.
1462|Cabinet and stability
1463|Policy → Cabinet: ministers are appointed according to the country's culture; a good minister boosts income, army and stability. War weariness and low stability cause unrest and rebellion in provinces; you can hire mercenaries to strengthen your army quickly.
1464|Declaring war
1465|First pick a weak neighboring country. My suggestion: {target}. Tap one of its provinces and press “Declare war”. Its allies may turn against you; weigh the cost.
1466|Tap an enemy province and press “Assault”. Pick your armies and choose the operation type (artillery, air, naval, missile, paradrop, special forces...). The advantage bar shows your chance of winning.
1467|Siege and conquest
1468|An army that beats the defense enters the province and besieges it; when the siege time runs out the province becomes yours and your war score rises. Armies need permission to cross borders.
1469|The peace table
1470|With a high war score, tap an enemy province and choose “Offer peace”: pick the provinces you take plus reparations, disarmament and buffer-zone terms using your score. Too many conquests raise “world reaction”; above 50 countries form a coalition.
1471|Alliances and relations
1472|Tap a friendly country's province: “Propose alliance” or “Relations” (who is allied with whom, who is at war). The handshake button on the right paints the map by relation colors.
1473|World and the UN
1474|The globe button on the right: rankings, blocs, news and the UN. In the UN you pass motions such as ceasefire, reconstruction, aid and sanctions by majority vote (no veto).
1475|Nuclear power and risks
1476|If you are a nuclear power you can launch a nuclear strike on an enemy province: massive destruction, but heavy sanctions and a coalition follow. Keep it as a last resort.
1477|Save and goals
1478|The ≡ menu at the top left: save/load, settings and “Goals and achievements”. You can enable autosave in settings. Achievements are permanent.
1479|You are ready!
1480|You are now ready for conquest, diplomacy or peaceful development. You can reopen the tutorial from the menu any time. Good luck!
